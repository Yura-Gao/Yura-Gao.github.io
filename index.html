About

I'm Yu (Yura) Gao, a Ph.D. student majoring in Computational Mathematics, Science and Engineering (CMSE) at Michigan State University. I work in the Hou Lab under the supervision of Dr. Jie Hou.

Before joining MSU, I received my M.S. in Applied Data Analytics from Boston University (2025). I earned my bachelor's degrees in Robotics Engineering from the University of Detroit Mercy and in Mechanical Design, Manufacturing and Automation from Beijing University of Chemical Technology (2023).

My research interests lie at the intersection of deep learning and structural biology, with a focus on developing AI and data-driven methods for RNA structure prediction, cryo-EM-assisted modeling, and structure quality assessment, and building LLM-based agents that automate structural analysis. Looking ahead, I am especially interested in structural representation learning and generative modeling for bioinformatics.

Research

ARSMA

Ongoing · Hou Lab

ARSMA (Advanced RNA Motif Structural Analysis) is an open-source, AI-driven platform for studying RNA structural motifs, the recurring building blocks of RNA 3D structure. It brings motif extraction, structural analysis, clustering, and cryo-EM density processing together in a single platform, with the goal of producing RNA motif data that is ready for AI and machine-learning applications.

The workflow is orchestrated by AutoRNA, a multi-agent system built on open-source large language models that runs the full CoSSMos workflow under a structured multi-agent governance loop. ARSMA is developed in the Hou Lab with support from an NIH/NIGMS R15 award on improving the AI readiness of RNA motif data.

Decision Support for Climate Risk Mitigation

2024 – 2025 · DSLab · BU

As a Research Assistant in the DSLab at Boston University, supervised by Prof. John Maleyeff, I built an R Shiny decision support tool that ranks mitigation strategies for university facilities using AHP, pairwise comparisons, and indifference probabilities, with time-series forecasts of high-wind events and color-coded cost-benefit matrices for facility managers.

AHP · time-series forecasting · R Shiny

Time-Series Trends of Medals

2024 – 2025

Analyzed 850+ medals from 1800 to 2025, using hierarchical clustering to trace shifts in material, size, and composition, and random forest and logistic regression models to classify medals as pre- or post-WWII with 87% accuracy.

hierarchical clustering · random forest · Python
