About

I'm Yura Gao, a first-year Ph.D. student majoring in Computational Mathematics, Science and Engineering (CMSE) at Michigan State University. I work in the Hou Lab under the supervision of Dr. Jie Hou.

Before joining MSU, I received my M.S. in Applied Data Analytics from Boston University (2025). I earned my bachelor's degrees in Robotics Engineering from the University of Detroit Mercy and in Mechanical Design, Manufacturing and Automation from Beijing University of Chemical Technology (2023).

My research interests lie at the intersection of machine learning and structural biology, with a focus on RNA. The Hou Lab develops deep learning and data-driven methods for protein and RNA structure prediction, cryo-EM-assisted modeling, and structure quality assessment, and is building LLM-based agents that automate structural analysis. Within this direction, I work on ARSMA, an open-source AI-driven platform for RNA motif structural analysis, where I lead the cryo-EM workflow for motif segmentation, and on MoTangler. Looking ahead, I am especially interested in structural representation learning and generative modeling for RNA 3D structure.

ARSMA

Ongoing · Hou Lab

An open-source, AI-driven platform for advanced RNA motif structural analysis. ARSMA brings together motif extraction, structural analysis, clustering, and cryo-EM processing, with the AutoRNA multi-agent system orchestrating the workflow.

Decision Support for Climate Risk Mitigation

2024 – 2025 · RA

Built an R Shiny decision support tool that ranks mitigation strategies for university facilities using AHP, pairwise comparisons, and indifference probabilities, with time-series forecasts of high-wind events and color-coded cost-benefit matrices for facility managers.

AHP · time-series forecasting · R Shiny

Time-Series Trends of Medals

2024 – 2025

Analyzed 850+ medals from 1800 to 2025, using hierarchical clustering to trace shifts in material, size, and composition, and random forest and logistic regression models to classify medals as pre- or post-WWII with 87% accuracy.

hierarchical clustering · random forest · Python
